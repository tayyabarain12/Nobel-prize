The Selection Process

Nobel Prize Nominations

Learn about the nomination process and how Nobel laureates are selected

How Are Nobel Laureates Selected?

1

Nomination

Qualified nominators submit candidates by January 31st each year. Only specific individuals, such as previous laureates, members of academies, university professors, and certain officials, can nominate candidates. Self-nominations are not permitted.

2

Review

Expert committees review all nominations and conduct thorough research on each candidate's contributions and impact on their respective fields. This process involves extensive evaluation and consultation with specialists.

3

Selection

Nobel Prize-awarding institutions vote to select the laureates. The selection process is confidential and takes place over several months. All deliberations and votes remain secret for 50 years.

4

Announcement

Winners are announced in October, with each category revealed on different days. The awards are presented on December 10th, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death, in Stockholm and Oslo.

Who Can Nominate?

Physics & Chemistry

  • Swedish and foreign members of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences
  • Nobel laureates in Physics and Chemistry
  • Members of the Nobel Committees
  • Professors at universities in Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, and Norway
  • Holders of corresponding chairs at selected universities worldwide

Physiology or Medicine

  • Members of the Nobel Assembly at Karolinska Institutet
  • Nobel laureates in Physiology or Medicine
  • Members of the Nobel Committee
  • Professors of medical sciences at Nordic universities
  • Holders of similar positions at selected universities worldwide

Literature

  • Members of the Swedish Academy
  • Members of other academies and societies similar to the Swedish Academy
  • Nobel laureates in Literature
  • Presidents of representative organizations of authors
  • Professors of literature and linguistics at universities

Peace

  • Members of national assemblies and governments
  • Members of international courts
  • Nobel Peace Prize laureates
  • Members of the Norwegian Nobel Committee
  • University professors of social sciences, history, philosophy, law, and theology

Important Dates

January 31

Nomination deadline - All nominations must be submitted by this date

February - September

Review and evaluation of nominations by expert committees

Early October

Announcement of Nobel Prize winners

December 10

Nobel Prize Award Ceremony in Stockholm and Oslo

Confidentiality

The statutes of the Nobel Foundation restrict disclosure of information about the nominations, whether publicly or privately, for 50 years. The restriction concerns the nominees and nominators, as well as investigations and opinions related to the award of a prize.

Important: Self-nominations are not accepted. Nominations cannot be made posthumously, but a deceased person may be awarded the prize if the nomination was made before February 1st of the year of their death.
