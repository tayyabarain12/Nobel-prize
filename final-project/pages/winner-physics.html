Nobel Prize in Physics

Honoring groundbreaking discoveries and innovations that have transformed our understanding of the physical universe

Recent Physics Laureates

2024

Pierre Agostini, Ferenc Krausz, Anne L'Huillier

"For experimental methods that generate attosecond pulses of light for the study of electron dynamics in matter"

The 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to three scientists who developed revolutionary methods to create extremely short pulses of light. These attosecond pulses (one attosecond is one billionth of a billionth of a second) allow scientists to observe and measure the rapid movements of electrons inside atoms and molecules.

Pierre Agostini (Ohio State University, USA) demonstrated a method to produce and investigate consecutive light pulses, each lasting only 250 attoseconds.

Ferenc Krausz (Max Planck Institute, Germany) isolated a single light pulse lasting 650 attoseconds, enabling precise measurements of electron behavior.

Anne L'Huillier (Lund University, Sweden) discovered that different overtones of light arise when laser light passes through a noble gas, laying the foundation for attosecond physics.

2023

Pierre Agostini, Ferenc Krausz, Anne L'Huillier

"For experimental methods that generate attosecond pulses of light"

These laureates have given humanity new tools for exploring the world of electrons inside atoms and molecules, opening up new possibilities in fields ranging from electronics to medicine.

2022

Alain Aspect, John F. Clauser, Anton Zeilinger

"For experiments with entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell inequalities and pioneering quantum information science"

The three laureates conducted groundbreaking experiments using entangled quantum states, where two particles behave like a single unit even when separated. Their results have paved the way for new technology based upon quantum information.

2021

Syukuro Manabe, Klaus Hasselmann, Giorgio Parisi

"For groundbreaking contributions to our understanding of complex physical systems"

Syukuro Manabe and Klaus Hasselmann laid the foundation of our knowledge of Earth's climate and how humanity influences it. Giorgio Parisi was recognized for his revolutionary contributions to the theory of disordered materials and random processes.

2020

Roger Penrose, Reinhard Genzel, Andrea Ghez

"For the discovery that black hole formation is a robust prediction of the general theory of relativity" and "for the discovery of a supermassive compact object at the centre of our galaxy"

Roger Penrose showed that black holes are a direct consequence of Einstein's general theory of relativity. Reinhard Genzel and Andrea Ghez discovered that an invisible and extremely heavy object governs the orbits of stars at the center of our galaxy - a supermassive black hole.
