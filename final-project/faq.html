Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about the Nobel Prize

General Questions

What is the Nobel Prize? ▼
The Nobel Prize is an international award administered by the Nobel Foundation in Stockholm, Sweden. It recognizes outstanding contributions in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences. The prizes were established by Alfred Nobel's will in 1895 and first awarded in 1901.
Who was Alfred Nobel? ▼
Alfred Nobel (1833-1896) was a Swedish chemist, engineer, inventor, and businessman. He is best known for inventing dynamite and holding 355 different patents. In his will, he dedicated his fortune to establish the Nobel Prizes to honor those who confer "the greatest benefit to humankind."
How many Nobel Prize categories are there? ▼
There are six Nobel Prize categories: Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences. The first five were established by Alfred Nobel's will in 1895. The Prize in Economic Sciences was established in 1968 by Sweden's central bank in memory of Alfred Nobel.
When are the Nobel Prizes announced? ▼
The Nobel Prizes are announced in early October each year, with each category announced on different days. The exact dates vary, but typically: Physiology or Medicine (first Monday), Physics (Tuesday), Chemistry (Wednesday), Literature (Thursday), Peace (Friday), and Economic Sciences (the following Monday).

Nomination & Selection

Who can nominate candidates for the Nobel Prize? ▼
Only specific individuals can nominate candidates, including previous Nobel laureates, members of the Nobel Committees, selected university professors, members of academies, and certain officials. The specific criteria vary by category. Self-nominations are not accepted.
Can the Nobel Prize be awarded posthumously? ▼
Since 1974, the Nobel Prize cannot be awarded posthumously. However, if a person is awarded the prize and dies before receiving it, the prize is still presented. A nominee who dies before the announcement can be awarded the prize if the nomination was made before February 1st of the year of their death.
How long are nomination records kept secret? ▼
Information about nominations is kept strictly confidential for 50 years. This restriction applies to nominees, nominators, and all investigations and opinions related to the award. After 50 years, the information becomes publicly available in the Nobel Prize database.

Prize Details

What do Nobel laureates receive? ▼
Each Nobel laureate receives three things: a Nobel diploma, a gold medal bearing Alfred Nobel's image, and a monetary award. The prize amount varies each year based on the Nobel Foundation's finances. In recent years, it has been approximately 11 million Swedish kronor (about $1 million USD).
Can the prize be shared? ▼
Yes, a Nobel Prize can be shared by up to three individuals. If there are two laureates, the prize is divided equally. If there are three, the prize can be divided equally or one laureate can receive half while the other two share the remaining half. Organizations can receive the Peace Prize without this limitation.
When and where is the Nobel Prize ceremony held? ▼
The Nobel Prize ceremony is held annually on December 10th, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death. The prizes in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, and Economic Sciences are awarded in Stockholm, Sweden. The Peace Prize is awarded in Oslo, Norway.

Statistics & Records

Who was the youngest Nobel laureate? ▼
Malala Yousafzai is the youngest Nobel laureate, receiving the Peace Prize in 2014 at the age of 17 for her struggle against the suppression of children and young people and for the right of all children to education.
How many women have won the Nobel Prize? ▼
As of 2024, 64 women have been awarded the Nobel Prize across all categories. Marie Curie was the first woman to win (Physics, 1903) and remains the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific categories (Physics 1903, Chemistry 1911).
Has anyone declined the Nobel Prize? ▼
Yes, two laureates have declined the Nobel Prize: Jean-Paul Sartre declined the Literature Prize in 1964, and Le Duc Tho declined the Peace Prize in 1973. Additionally, four laureates were forced by their governments to decline: Richard Kuhn (Chemistry, 1938), Adolf Butenandt (Chemistry, 1939), Gerhard Domagk (Medicine, 1939), and Boris Pasternak (Literature, 1958).
How many times has the Nobel Prize not been awarded? ▼
The Nobel Prize has not been awarded 49 times, mostly during World War I (1914-1918) and World War II (1939-1945). If no suitable candidate is found or if the situation in the world makes it inappropriate to award the prize, it can be reserved until the following year or not awarded at all.
